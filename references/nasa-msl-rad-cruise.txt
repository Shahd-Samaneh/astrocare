title: Measurements of Energetic Particle Radiation in Transit to Mars on the Mars Science Laboratory (Zeitlin et al., Science, 31 May 2013; NASA/JPL release 30 May 2013)
url: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1235989
id: nasa-msl-rad-cruise

Summary written by the AstroCare team in our own words (not copied text). Sources: Zeitlin et al.,
Science 340(6136), 31 May 2013, the NASA/JPL news release of 30 May 2013 on the same result,
and the AAAS/Science summary of 30 May 2013.

- The Radiation Assessment Detector (RAD) on NASA's Mars Science Laboratory (Curiosity) measured
  radiation during the 2011-2012 cruise from Earth to Mars.
- Averaged over the cruise, the dose equivalent was about 1.8 mSv per day.
- RAD was inside the spacecraft during cruise, so this rate already includes that vehicle's
  shielding. A different vehicle, or a different point in the solar cycle, would give a different rate.
- Most of the cruise dose came from galactic cosmic rays. Solar particle events added about 5% of
  the total.
- The measurements represent low-to-moderate solar activity (AAAS/Science summary, 30 May 2013).

How AstroCare uses it: as a simulated daily rate for a deep-space transit mission
(config/thresholds.yaml: radiation_deep_space_rate_msv_per_day). It is an estimate, not a
dosimeter reading.
